How to Pick a Good Time to Move or Sign a Contract with Qimen Dunjia

To pick a time, narrow your options first. Write down the days you’re actually available and the hours that work on each day. Then compare every two-hour period in that range. Look at the three smoothest slots and the two to avoid. For moving, look at the Life Door. For signing, look at Six Harmony. If another person is involved, compare where each of you falls in the chart for that period. Here’s how to do it.

1. Start with the times that work

Whatever method you use to pick a time, start with your real-world constraints. A slot won’t help if the movers have no truck that day or the other person is unavailable.

  • Moving: Your closing or lease start date, when you must leave your old place, and the dates the movers are available.
  • Signing: When the other person is available, the earliest date your documents will be ready, and any payment or signing deadline.
  • Hours of the day: If you can only sign during business hours, limit your search to daytime. Moves are usually scheduled during the day too.

Once you’ve written those down, you’ll usually have a few days and several hours on each day to choose from. The clearer the range, the easier it is to use the results.

2. Calendar dates vs. two-hour periods

One common approach is to check a traditional Chinese calendar for dates marked suitable for moving into a home or signing a contract. That works by the day. Everyone sees the same guidance for that date, though some people also check which Chinese zodiac animal clashes with it.

Hour-based Qimen Dunjia works by two-hour period. It makes a chart for each period, then looks at what appears in the palace representing you. It addresses a different question: “Which period within these days looks smoother for me?”

You can use both approaches. First, narrow the dates using the method that matters to you. Then compare the two-hour periods on those dates. Chengshi does not use the traditional calendar’s suitable or unsuitable activities. It makes hour-based Qimen Dunjia charts.

3. What to look at for moving or signing

For each two-hour period, start with the Door in your palace. The Open, Rest, and Life Doors count as Smooth. The Block, Harm, Fear, and Death Doors count as Avoid. I explain the Eight Doors in this introduction to Qimen Dunjia.

Traditionally, you also look at something tied to the kind of matter at hand. Chengshi uses these associations.

If a specific person is involved, such as a landlord, buyer, or business partner, Chengshi also compares how the palaces representing each of you interact in that period. Its scoring method assigns +2 if they support you, +1 if you restrain them, +0.5 if you are on equal footing, −1 if you support them, and −2 if they restrain you. It adds +1 if they fall in a favorable Door or −0.5 if they fall in an unfavorable Door. You can still add someone without their birth date. Choose your relationship to them, such as “Other: client, other party,” and Chengshi uses a traditional method to select a chart symbol to represent them.

4. Use Chengshi’s “Pick a Time” step by step

  1. Open the “Ask” tab and tap “Pick a Time.”
  2. Under “What do you want to do,” describe the matter, such as “12/5-12/10 move.” If you include dates or times, such as today, tomorrow afternoon, next Wednesday, or 12/5-12/10, the range below updates to match. If you mention moving or looking at a home, Chengshi initially selects “Buying or renting a home.” You can change it.
  3. Under “Which days,” choose a start and end date. The range can be 1 to 31 days. There are also shortcuts for 3, 7, and 14 days.
  4. Under “Which part of the day,” choose the hours you’re available. The default is 9:00 to 21:00.
  5. Confirm “What kind of matter.” Under “Who it involves,” select a person if there is one. Otherwise, leave it blank.

When you submit, Chengshi makes a chart for every two-hour period in the range and opens the results. The calculations happen on your iPhone, without an internet connection.

5. Read and use the results

The results appear as a color-coded grid, with dates across and two-hour periods down. A total of 2 or more is Smooth (green). −2 or less is Avoid (red). Scores in between are So-so (yellow). These thresholds are a little stricter than the 1.5 threshold on the home screen’s period dial because this grid is meant for planning an important matter.

  • The three highest-scoring squares have stars and appear under “Three best time periods.”
  • “To avoid” lists the two most difficult slots in the range.
  • Tap any square to see the point-by-point calculation below it. You can also tap “View full chart for this period.” Full charts for today and tomorrow are free. Charts for other dates require Pro. “Pick a Time” itself is free.

If every period in your range scores poorly, Chengshi says so. It suggests choosing the lightest red square or pushing the dates later. If you search only one day and all its periods score poorly, it also shows a smoother slot within the following seven days as another option.

Moving takes all day. My own approach is to save the smoothest slot for the step I care about most, such as signing for the keys or bringing the first boxes inside. For a contract, I simply schedule the signing in that slot. Chengshi saves the “Pick a Time” record in the matter’s folder. Afterward, you can mark “What happened next?” Under “Review afterward” on the “Ask” tab, Chengshi separates matters where Xiaoqi mostly said “Go ahead” from those where Xiaoqi said “Wait,” then compares the proportions that went smoothly.

If you just want to ask, “Should I have this discussion now?” use “Ask Now.” It makes a chart for the moment you tap and checks whether there are notably better daytime periods in the next three days. For more on the daily periods, see the twelve two-hour periods guide. You can find the app’s full feature list on the Chengshi home page.

6. Common questions

I can only move on one day. Is it still worth picking a time?

Yes. Select one day, and Chengshi compares each two-hour period on that day. You can use the smoothest slot for the most important step. If all the periods score poorly, it suggests the lightest red square and shows a smoother slot within the following seven days for comparison.

What if I don’t know my landlord’s or the other person’s birth date?

That’s okay. When you add a person, choose “I don’t know” for their birth date. Then select your relationship to them, such as “Other: client, other party.” Chengshi uses a traditional method to select a chart symbol to represent them.

The traditional calendar marks a day as suitable for moving, but Chengshi says Avoid. Which should I use?

They look at different things. The traditional calendar gives everyone the same guidance about activities on a given day. Chengshi compares that day’s two-hour periods for you. You can use the calendar to choose a date, then use Chengshi to compare periods on that date.

How many days can I compare at once?

1 to 31 days. If you enter a range longer than 31 days under “What do you want to do,” Chengshi stops at day 31 and tells you.

Do I have to pay to pick a time?

No. “Ask Now,” “Pick a Time,” and matter folders are free. Pro adds lessons 6 through 17, charts for dates other than today and tomorrow, and the full conclusions and suggestions in “Track Record.”
