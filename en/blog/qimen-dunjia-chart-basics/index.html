How to Read a Qimen Dunjia Chart: Palaces, Doors, and Signs

Hour-based Qimen Dunjia makes one chart for each two-hour period, or twelve charts a day. Start with the directions of the Nine Palaces. Then look at the Eight Doors, the Chief, and a few other signs. Finally, find the palace that represents you. This guide follows those steps, with a table for each one. It ends with how to view a chart on your iPhone.

1. What is in an hour-based Qimen chart

A Qimen Dunjia chart has nine squares called the Nine Palaces. Each palace holds a Door from the Eight Doors, a Star from the Nine Stars, a Spirit from the Eight Spirits, and Heavenly Stems on two layers: the Heaven Plate and the Earth Plate.

Hour-based Qimen makes a new chart every two-hour period. Morning and afternoon charts on the same day differ. There are two main ways to arrange a chart: rotating-plate and flying-plate. Chengshi uses rotating-plate. There are also two methods for setting up the chart, Chaibu and Zhirun. They produce different charts for more than 30% of two-hour periods. Chengshi defaults to Chaibu, and you can switch to Zhirun in Settings.

You don’t need to memorize everything at once. Follow these four steps to get a general picture of a two-hour period.

2. Step 1: Find the directions of the Nine Palaces

In a traditional chart, south is at the top, north at the bottom, east on the left, and west on the right. That is the reverse of the usual map orientation. Each palace has a number, a name, and an element.

3. Step 2: Look at the Eight Doors

Think of the Eight Doors as eight kinds of situations. Each Door has a home palace and an element. Every two-hour period, all eight Doors rotate together to new positions.

Traditional descriptions often group three Doors as favorable and three as unfavorable, with View and Block usually treated as neutral. Chengshi puts Block in the Avoid group because it represents obstruction and makes it harder to move forward. That is a choice in Chengshi’s scoring method. The Door carries the most weight in a palace, so start there for a general sense of it.

4. Step 3: Find the Chief and other signs

The Nine Stars are Tianpeng, Tianrui, Tianchong, Tianfu, Tianqin, Tianxin, Tianzhu, Tianren, and Tianying. The Eight Spirits are Chief, Serpent, Moon, Six Harmony, White Tiger, Black Tortoise, Nine Earth, and Nine Heaven. To get started, learn to spot the Chief and these five other signs.

SignMeaningHow to find it
ChiefFirst of the Eight Spirits. The palace it occupies has more influence over the pace.Marked directly on the chart.
VoidLess ability to act.Find the cycle that contains this two-hour period. The two Earthly Branches without matching Heavenly Stems are Void. For example, Xu and Hai are unmatched in the Jiazi cycle.
Horse StarQuick movement.Use the period’s Earthly Branch: Shen, Zi, Chen point to Yin; Yin, Wu, Xu point to Shen; Si, You, Chou point to Hai; and Hai, Mao, Wei point to Si.
Door ClashReduces the benefit of a favorable Door or adds to the difficulty of an unfavorable one.The Door’s element restrains the element of its palace. For example, Open Door (Metal) in Zhen palace (Wood).
PunishmentThings may get stuck.One of the six markers reaches a palace with which it has a punishment relationship. For example, Wu (戊, standing for Jiazi) in Zhen palace (Mao), where Zi and Mao punish each other.
TombHarder to move a matter forward.A Heavenly Stem enters its Tomb palace: Yi, Bing, and Wu (戊) in Qian; Ding, Ji, and Geng in Gen; Xin and Ren in Xun; Gui in Kun.

Traditional descriptions include Fanyin (reversal), Door Control, and Five Unmet Hours. Chengshi currently uses only the signs above to adjust the score: Chief +1, Void −1, Horse Star +0.5, and Door Clash, Punishment, and Tomb −1 each.

5. Step 4: Find your palace

Which palace represents you? Use your birth-year stem, the Heavenly Stem of the year you were born. The year changes at Lichun, a solar term around February 4. If you were born in January or early February, check whether your birthday fell before Lichun. If it did, use the previous year. Your birth-year stem is not your zodiac animal or your Day Master. You only need the year’s Heavenly Stem.

If that stem is Jia, there is one extra step. Jia does not appear directly on the chart. It is hidden beneath one of six markers. This is where the name “Dunjia” comes from.

JiaziJiaxuJiashenJiawuJiachenJiayin
Wu 戊Ji 己Geng 庚Xin 辛Ren 壬Gui 癸

For example, 1984 was a Jiazi year. To find your palace, look for Wu (戊) on the chart. Then look at that palace’s Door and signs to get a sense of the period. If your birth-year stem lands in the center, it is called “entering the center.” The center has no Door. Its Tianqin Star is lodged in the palace that holds the Tianrui Star. Chengshi places your birth-year stem there too when calculating the score.

Do this for each period, and you can color all twelve periods of the day. I explain how Chengshi turns the points into green, yellow, and red in the guide to the twelve two-hour periods.

7. Common questions

How are Qimen Dunjia and BaZi different?

BaZi uses a person’s birth year, month, day, and time to describe that person. Hour-based Qimen uses the chart for a particular two-hour period to describe that point in time. Chengshi uses both: BaZi for daily energy and hour-based Qimen for each two-hour period’s Smooth, So-so, or Avoid label.

How often does an hour-based Qimen chart change?

Every two-hour period. That makes twelve charts a day.

Should I choose Chaibu or Zhirun?

Both methods are used, and they produce different charts for more than 30% of two-hour periods. If you’re unsure, use Chaibu, Chengshi’s default. To switch, go to “Settings → Advanced → How charts are made.”

My birth-year stem is Jia. Why can’t I find it on the chart?

Jia is hidden beneath one of six markers: Jiazi uses Wu, Jiaxu uses Ji, Jiashen uses Geng, Jiawu uses Xin, Jiachen uses Ren, and Jiayin uses Gui. Find the cycle for your birth year, then look for its marker.

Is Qimen Dunjia useful?

That depends on what useful means to you. Chengshi shows each point it adds or subtracts. If you make an optional nightly check-in about your day, “Track Record” puts the app’s suggestions next to your notes and compares them with a random baseline. You can review the match rate using your own records.
