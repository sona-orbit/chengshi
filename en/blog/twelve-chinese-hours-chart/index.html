1. The twelve two-hour periods

Traditionally, a day is divided into twelve periods of two hours each. Each is named for an Earthly Branch. The branches also correspond to the twelve zodiac animals and the five elements. That’s why you may see the Wu period described as the Horse period, associated with Fire.

An easy way to remember the times: every period starts at an odd-numbered hour. Wu runs from 11:00 to 13:00. Move forward or back two hours at a time to find the others.

2. Three common points of confusion

Does Zi belong to today or tomorrow?

Zi crosses midnight, so there are two ways to assign its date. Under one common method, the day changes at 23:00. For someone born at 23:30, the Day Pillar is the next day’s pillar. Another method changes the day at 0:00. It splits Zi into late Zi (23:00–24:00, assigned to the current day) and early Zi (0:00–1:00, assigned to the next day).

Both methods are used. Chengshi changes the day at 23:00. If you open the app after 23:00, “Now” appears on tomorrow’s period chart.

Each period starts at an odd-numbered hour

It’s easy to mistake Zi for 0:00 to 2:00. It actually starts at 23:00 on the previous day. Chou runs from 1:00 to 3:00. Check the odd-numbered start time when using the table.

Phone time and true solar time

Your phone shows standard time for its time zone. China and Taiwan both use UTC+8, with a standard meridian at 120 degrees east. Each degree of longitude changes solar time by about 4 minutes. Taipei is near 121.5 degrees east, so its local mean solar time is about 6 minutes ahead of phone time. Add the equation of time, which shifts over the year, and the gap can sometimes reach about twenty minutes.

This usually makes little difference when planning your day. It matters most in the minutes around the boundary between periods. Chengshi uses your phone’s time zone and clock time. It does not convert to true solar time. If a time falls right on a boundary, you can check both neighboring periods.

4. How Chengshi colors each period

For each two-hour period, Chengshi makes a Qimen Dunjia chart. It finds the palace in the Nine Palaces that holds the Heavenly Stem of your birth year, called your birth-year stem, and checks what else is there. Here is how the points are added and subtracted.

  • The Eight Doors carry the most weight: Open, Rest, and Life Door are each +2; View Door is 0; Block, Harm, Fear, and Death Door are each −2.
  • Chief in your palace is +1, Void is −1, and Horse Star is +0.5.
  • Door Clash, Punishment, and Tomb are each −1.

A total of 1.5 or higher is green (Smooth). A total of −1.5 or lower is red (Avoid). Anything between is yellow (So-so). For example, suppose your palace has Open Door and Chief but is also in Void: 2＋1−1＝2. That period is green.

Block Door is traditionally often treated as neutral. I count it as Avoid in Chengshi because it represents obstruction and difficulty moving forward. That is a choice in Chengshi’s scoring method. Another tool might call the same period So-so while Chengshi calls it Avoid. Tap any period in the app to see its points. I explain the Eight Doors and Nine Palaces in my introduction to Qimen Dunjia charts.

5. Using the colors to plan your day

The “Tomorrow” tab in Chengshi has a twelve-section dial. You can see the color of each period for the next day the night before. Here’s how I use it.

  • If I can choose when to have a discussion, submit work, or make a decision, I pick a green period.
  • If something I can’t move falls in a red period, I leave more time and room in my schedule.
  • Zi, Chou, and Yin (23:00 to 5:00) appear lighter because I’m usually asleep then. The home screen’s “Smoothest” picks only from Mao through Hai.

Red means a period may feel more difficult, not that something will go wrong. Green means it may feel smoother, not a promise of success. What happens still depends on what you do. For a matter like moving or signing a contract, you can use “Pick a Time” to compare periods across several days. I explain how in how to pick a time for moving or signing.

6. Common questions

What time is Zi?

Zi runs from 23:00 to 01:00 the next morning. It is the first of the twelve two-hour periods. Each following period lasts two hours and starts at an odd-numbered hour.

If I was born after 23:00, which day is my Day Pillar?

It depends on the method. A day change at 23:00 assigns the next day’s pillar. With a day change at 0:00, a birth between 23:00 and 24:00 still belongs to the current day. Chengshi changes the day at 23:00.

How long is a two-hour period?

Two hours. The twelve periods make up a full 24-hour day.

Are the period ratings the same every day?

No. The traditional calendar’s ratings change with the day’s Heavenly Stem and Earthly Branches. In the birth-year method, each period has its own chart. The same period can therefore have a different rating today and tomorrow.

Does Chengshi convert to true solar time?

No. Chengshi uses your phone’s time zone and clock time. If a time falls right on the boundary between two periods, you can check both.
